What the position actually gives a partner

  • Access before the process. The best catalogs, rosters and partnerships are shown to known parties first. Backseat is one of those parties — most opportunities arrive before a banker is retained.
  • Underwriting that is not guesswork. Fifteen years of watching how records actually earn is a valuation instrument. Decay curves, sync viability, re-record risk and the difference between a catalog that is stable and one that is merely quiet.
  • Execution by people who have executed. Marketing counsel from operators who have run campaigns at scale, not from a deck built for the pitch.
  • One call instead of six months. The introduction that takes a new entrant two quarters to earn is, here, a phone call — and it is made on a reputation that took fifteen years to build and would take one bad act to lose.

Why we are a different counterparty

  • We are operators who became owners. Backseat buys and builds the assets it advises on. Our judgement is tested with our own capital, not only with a client's.
  • The relationship outlives the transaction. A single deal is worth less to us than a standing in the business we intend to hold for another fifteen years. That asymmetry is the client's protection.
  • One relationship, three depths. Advisory, services and outright acquisition all sit inside the same group — so a conversation can move from a fee to a joint venture to a sale without changing counterparties.
  • Discretion is a default. Much of the best work in this business cannot be published. We are comfortable operating where credit is neither given nor sought.

What the balance sheet changes

  • Capital can move with the advice. When the right answer is to buy the asset rather than write about it, that is available in the same conversation.
  • Alignment can be structural. Equity, profit share and co-investment are on the table, so incentives can be built into the deal instead of policed by the contract.
  • Speed where it is scarce. Decisions are made by principals. There is no committee between a partner and an answer.
  • Focus. We do two things — buy catalogs and service them. A partner is not paying for attention that is spread across unrelated businesses.

How capital is disciplined

Backseat applies one governing test to every dollar it deploys, on its own account and alongside partners: it must do at least two of the following. A partner should expect to be asked which two.

The venture standpoint is already in the ground rather than in a plan: two music catalogs under active review, a services and advisory book that can be opened on relationships alone, and Vyb — a product live in market and earning today. A partner is joining something that is already moving.
